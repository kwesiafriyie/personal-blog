## Recently, I’ve Been Looking Into Go

lately, i’ve been spending some time getting familiar with go.

not because i suddenly want to rewrite everything in a new language.

and not because i think every engineer should learn go.

it mostly started from a different interest entirely — site reliability engineering.

---

## Following Curiosity

the more i read about sre, infrastructure, reliability, distributed systems, observability, and platform engineering, the more one thing kept appearing.

go.

not every time.

but enough times to make me curious.

tools i found interesting.
platform discussions.
engineering blogs.
internal tooling conversations.

go kept showing up.

---

## I’m Not Learning It Like a Language Course

i’m not approaching it with the goal of mastering syntax first.

right now, i’m mostly trying to understand:

- how go thinks
- why infrastructure teams seem to like it
- how concurrency actually feels in practice
- what makes it attractive for backend and systems work

less “complete the tutorial.”

more “build small things and see what happens.”

---

## It Feels Different

coming from environments where i’ve worked more around automation, integrations, systems, and business processes, go feels refreshingly straightforward.

there’s something interesting about a language that doesn’t seem interested in impressing you.

---

## No Big Goals Yet

i don’t have some huge roadmap and i’m not trying to become an sre overnight, i’m mostly exploring.

reading.
experimenting.
breaking things.
understanding why things break.

and seeing where that leads.

---

## Final Thoughts

i’ve realized some of the best things i’ve learned started from curiosity instead of plans.

so for now, i’m just following the interest.

maybe it becomes something bigger.

maybe it stays as exploration.

either way, i’m enjoying learning again.
